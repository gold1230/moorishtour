# How to Get a Tourist Visa to the USA from Morocco: Complete 2026 Guide

**Updated October 10, 2026 | Originally published July 2, 2025 | By Moorish Tour**

A Moroccan citizen who wants to visit the United States for a holiday, family visit or short tourist trip normally needs a **B-2 visitor visa**, often issued as a combined **B1/B2 visa**. The main steps are simple to understand: complete the official DS-160 application, pay the required fee, book an appointment using the U.S. visa appointment service for Morocco, attend any required interview at the U.S. Consulate General in Casablanca, and wait for the consular decision. The difficult part is not memorizing these steps. It is preparing truthful information that explains why you want to visit, how you can pay for the journey, and why your visit is temporary.

This guide from Moorish Tour explains the process in clear English for people living in Morocco. It covers the DS-160, documents, fees, interview questions, common mistakes and what happens after approval or refusal. The information is based primarily on official U.S. Department of State guidance. **Moorish Tour is a Morocco travel company, not the U.S. government, a consulate, or a visa-approval service.** Always follow the instructions provided by the U.S. Consulate and its appointment system for your own application.

## 1. Do Moroccan Citizens Need a Visa to Visit the United States?

Yes. A person traveling on an ordinary Moroccan passport generally must obtain a U.S. visitor visa before going to the United States for tourism. Morocco is not a participant in the U.S. Visa Waiver Program, so Moroccan passport holders should not plan to use ESTA as a replacement for a B-2 visa. Travelers who hold another passport may have different rules according to their nationality and personal circumstances.

A B-2 visa is for activities such as a vacation, sightseeing, visiting family and friends, or certain medical visits. You might hope to see New York, Washington, Miami or another American city. You can explain that trip in ordinary language without producing a complicated professional itinerary. It is more important that your purpose matches the visa category and is consistent with your application.

A visitor visa is different from permission to work, study toward a degree or immigrate to the United States. If your real purpose is employment, university study or permanent settlement, you should investigate the appropriate visa instead of describing the trip as tourism. Read the official LINK[U.S. Department of State visitor visa information|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html] before starting.

NOTE[Quick answer]A U.S. tourist visa is for a temporary visit. It is not a work permit, a residency document or a guarantee of entry at the airport.

## 2. B-2 vs. B1/B2 Visa: Which One Should You Choose?

The U.S. system uses **B-1** for certain temporary business visits and **B-2** for tourism, family visits and medical treatment. Many approved applicants receive a combined B1/B2 visa. For example, someone attending a short professional meeting and then taking a few days of holiday may fall into a combination of permitted activities. Someone visiting a relative for two weeks is normally describing a B-2 purpose.

The difference matters because a visitor visa does not authorize ordinary employment in the United States. It also does not replace a student visa for a full course of academic study. Temporary business discussions and conferences may be permitted under B-1, but actually working for a U.S. employer is a different matter. Applicants should describe the intended activity accurately rather than choosing a category because they think one is easier to obtain.

TABLE[Visa category|Suitable examples|Not suitable for]
B-2 visitor|Holiday, sightseeing, family or friend visits, certain medical treatment|Taking a U.S. job or moving permanently
B-1 business visitor|Business meetings, some conferences and contract discussions|Regular paid employment in the United States
B1/B2 combined|Temporary travel involving permitted business and tourism activities|Activities requiring a separate visa category

If you are unsure, use the official LINK[visitor visa activity examples|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html] rather than advice from a social-media video. Your actual purpose of travel matters more than a label suggested by a third party.

## 4. Who Can Qualify for a U.S. Visitor Visa in Morocco?

The consular officer decides whether an applicant qualifies under U.S. immigration law. For a temporary visitor visa, an important issue is whether the person has a credible reason to return outside the United States after the trip. This is often described as showing ties to Morocco, but no one document automatically proves the case and no private travel agency can guarantee the outcome.

Relevant circumstances may include stable work, continuing studies, an established business, close family responsibilities, a home, or other meaningful commitments. The officer also considers whether the stated duration and expenses fit the applicant's situation. A person with a modest income can still have a genuine reason for a short trip; a large bank balance alone does not guarantee approval. Information is examined as a whole.

Applicants who operate a small business, work seasonally, freelance, study, or are supported by family should describe their situation honestly. For example, a self-employed shop owner might bring genuine registration or business documents if useful, while a student may be able to explain the timing of classes and examinations. Do not invent employment, fabricate reservations or buy property simply to create an appearance of ties.

NOTE[What really matters]An officer assesses the whole application. Having a job, a bank account, a sponsor, or an invitation letter does not create automatic eligibility.

## 5. How to Complete the DS-160 Form Correctly

The DS-160 is the online application for a temporary U.S. visa. It asks for identity details, passport information, travel purpose, employment and education history, previous travel, security questions and other personal background. Write names and dates carefully so they match your documents. If you have changed your name or have prior visas, answer the relevant questions accurately.

Before beginning, gather your passport, basic details of your intended trip, accommodation or U.S. contact information if known, work or study history, and previous international travel details. Save your application ID so you can return to an unfinished form when the system permits. Review every page rather than rushing to the final submission screen. Inconsistencies between the DS-160 and your interview answers can create unnecessary problems.

After submission, print or save the DS-160 **barcode confirmation page**; the U.S. government says the complete application does not need to be printed for the interview. Upload the photo as instructed, and follow the official requirements if the upload fails. The Department of State explains these points in its LINK[DS-160 frequently asked questions|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application/ds-160-faqs.html].

BULLETS[Use your real passport name and correct date of birth|Choose the truthful purpose and expected duration of travel|Provide accurate employment, education and travel history|Save the application ID and final confirmation page|Never claim false relatives, fake employment or invented travel history|Review the photo requirements before submitting]

If you discover a mistake after submission, consult the official instructions rather than modifying an appointment record informally. In some situations a new DS-160 may be necessary, and the appointment system may need the updated barcode number.

## 6. Where to Book the U.S. Visa Interview in Morocco

Routine nonimmigrant visa interviews for applicants in Morocco are handled by the U.S. Consulate General in **Casablanca**. Applicants may travel there from cities such as Rabat, Marrakech, Fes, Tangier, Agadir, or other areas. Do not assume you can attend a routine tourist-visa interview at the U.S. Embassy in Rabat. Follow the instructions displayed by the official appointment service for Morocco.

The LINK[Embassy of Morocco's U.S. visa application overview|https://us.diplomatie.ma/en/how-apply-nonimmigrant-us-visa] describes the appointment process and points applicants to the Morocco service at LINK[U.S. Travel Docs Morocco|https://www.ustraveldocs.com/ma/en/nonimmigrant-visa]. Scheduling accounts, payment methods, pickup rules and appointment availability can change, so use the current official portal for the exact sequence.

Plan transportation and an overnight stay only if you genuinely need one. Keep in mind that arriving for an interview does not guarantee a decision that day or immediate return of your passport. Review the consulate's security notice carefully: restrictions on phones, large bags and electronic items may apply. Do not rely on an unofficial agent's promised appointment date.

## 7. U.S. Tourist Visa Fee in Morocco and Possible Extra Charges

The published standard application processing fee for B visitor visas is **US$185**, generally non-refundable even if the application is refused. The method of payment and the amount shown in Moroccan dirhams are set through the local appointment process, so avoid relying on an old exchange rate shown in a blog post or video. Check the portal before paying, keep your receipt, and never send the fee to a personal account.

The U.S. reciprocity schedule lists **no additional reciprocity issuance fee** for B-1, B-2 or B1/B2 visas issued to Moroccan nationals. This is separate from the application fee. U.S. law also provides for a visa integrity fee on nonimmigrant visa issuance, and implementation or collection arrangements may change. Check the official government and payment instructions for any amounts actually due at the time of your application or issuance; do not assume every statutory fee is already collected in the same way.

## 9. Bank Statements, Income and Sponsorship: What Is Enough?

There is **no single official minimum bank balance** that automatically qualifies every tourist visa applicant. The relevant question is whether the planned trip is financially believable given its length, activities and the resources available. A short visit with a reasonable budget may look very different from a long and expensive multi-city holiday.

Prepare genuine records that reflect your normal financial situation. Large deposits just before the interview may invite additional questions if they do not match your income; do not borrow money temporarily simply to display a higher balance. If someone else intends to pay some costs, explain the relationship and proposed arrangement truthfully, and bring supporting material if appropriate.

A friend or relative in the United States can sometimes help with accommodation, but a host does not take the place of your own eligibility. The State Department specifically explains that an invitation letter or Form I-134 affidavit of support is **not required** for a visitor visa and is not itself a deciding factor. Read the official LINK[visitor-visa document guidance|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html] rather than online claims about a fixed bank amount.

QUOTE[There is no magic bank balance. A realistic trip and honest explanation are more useful than a financial figure chosen to impress an interviewer.]

## 10. U.S. Tourist Visa Interview Questions and Helpful Preparation

A consular interview is usually focused on straightforward matters: why you want to travel, where you will go, how long you intend to stay, what you do in Morocco, how the trip will be funded and whether you have traveled before. The questions vary with the application. Some interviews are short; others require more detail. Being asked few questions does not automatically mean either approval or refusal.

Answer in your own words. If the officer asks how long you will stay, a clear answer such as 'about ten days, mainly visiting my sister and seeing the city' is more useful than a speech memorized from a video. If you do not understand a question, calmly ask for it to be repeated or clarified. Never contradict a truthful DS-160 merely because you think a different answer sounds better.

TABLE[Example interview question|What a clear answer should explain]

## 11. Applicants Without a Job, Students and Self-Employed Moroccans

People sometimes assume that anyone without a formal monthly salary will automatically be refused. U.S. immigration law does not use such a simple rule. The consular officer assesses the individual situation, temporary purpose, financial resources and reasons to leave the United States after the visit. A student, self-employed person, retiree or parent may be able to explain genuine circumstances without claiming employment they do not have.

A student could describe their school or university program, upcoming classes and who is paying for the vacation. A self-employed applicant might explain the business activity and how it is managed. A parent or spouse may describe their real responsibilities and the planned family trip. Do not misrepresent unemployment, income, marital status or the reason for visiting America.

## 12. Visiting Family or Friends in the USA: Do You Need an Invitation?

Visiting a sibling, cousin, parent or friend can be a normal B-2 travel purpose. Be honest about whom you will see, how you know them, their city and whether they will provide accommodation or help with costs. You can bring contact details and a basic travel outline if relevant. The key is that the trip should be temporary and correctly described.

An invitation letter can be useful for your personal planning, but it is **not a mandatory approval document** for a standard B visitor visa. The government's guidance explicitly says that applicants qualify based on their own circumstances and ties abroad rather than assurances from friends or family in the United States. Paying an agent to write a polished invitation letter is not a reliable way to obtain a visa.

A genuine visit may include time sightseeing around your host's city. Be clear about the likely dates and budget, but do not buy costly non-refundable tickets merely to show the officer a flight reservation. Official guidance says there is no guarantee of issuance and recommends waiting until the visa is received before final travel commitments.

## 13. U.S. Tourist Visa for a Married Couple, Children or a Family

If a couple or family wants to travel together, each person who needs a visa must have an individual application. A child's application is not automatically approved because a parent has a visa. Fees, passports and any required interviews or additional steps should be checked separately for each applicant. The appointment portal may support family scheduling, but it still needs each applicant's details.

Current State Department guidance generally requires an in-person interview for nonimmigrant visa applicants, including children under 14 and adults over 79, except for limited listed exemptions. Old travel articles often say small children never attend interviews, but that broad claim is no longer dependable. Always verify the rules for each family member before the appointment.

Consider the practical needs of children when traveling to Casablanca: the travel time, snacks, weather and instructions about what can enter the consulate matter. If you are applying as a group, be consistent about trip duration and who is paying for the holiday. A family application does not remove the requirement to qualify for the correct visa.

## 14. Can You Renew a U.S. Tourist Visa Without an Interview?

Some people renewing an existing B-1, B-2 or B1/B2 visa may qualify for an interview waiver, but it is not a general rule for everyone. Under the State Department update effective October 1, 2025, certain adult applicants renewing a full-validity visitor visa **within 12 months of its expiration** may be eligible when other conditions are met. The applicant generally must have been at least 18 when the earlier visa was issued, apply in the country of nationality or usual residence, and meet the refusal and ineligibility criteria.

Even an applicant who appears to meet those conditions may be asked to attend an interview. Do not post your passport to an unofficial address or assume 'dropbox' procedures work the same way as they did years ago. Use the current official LINK[interview-waiver guidance|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/interview-waiver-update-sept-18-2025.html] and follow the Morocco appointment portal's individual instructions.

NOTE[Renewal is not automatic]Having held a U.S. visa before does not guarantee another one. Keep accurate application information and follow the current interview-waiver eligibility checks.

Apply comfortably ahead of your desired departure rather than booking a non-refundable flight first. If you have a serious urgent reason to travel, read the official appointment service for any stated expedited procedure. Do not trust paid advertisements promising same-day appointments or automatic approval. Keep your plans flexible until the passport is returned.

## 16. Why a Tourist Visa May Be Refused: 214(b) and 221(g)

Some visa refusals occur under **section 214(b)** of U.S. immigration law. In broad terms, the officer was not satisfied that the applicant had established eligibility for the requested temporary visa. This may involve questions about the trip purpose, ties outside the United States or the applicant's overall circumstances. It is not necessarily an accusation of dishonesty and there is no universal document that reverses this decision.

A case handled under **section 221(g)** may involve missing documents, incomplete information or additional administrative processing. Follow any written instructions carefully, because the appropriate next step depends on what the consular officer asked for. A refusal under 221(g) should not be treated as identical to a 214(b) decision.

A person refused under 214(b) may generally reapply, but must submit a new application and usually pay the application fee again. Reapplying immediately with the same information is not a reliable strategy. Consider whether your circumstances have materially changed, and do not provide false documents or misrepresent facts in an effort to improve your chances. For detailed legal explanations, consult the Department of State's LINK[visa denials guide|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html].

## 17. Moroccan B1/B2 Visa Validity: Does Ten Years Mean a Ten-Year Stay?

The current U.S. reciprocity schedule lists Moroccan B-1, B-2 and B1/B2 visas as potentially valid for **120 months (ten years), with multiple entries** and no additional reciprocity issuance fee. That describes a standard validity schedule for eligible visa issuance, not a personal promise that every applicant will receive exactly that validity. The visa printed in the passport controls the actual dates and entries for that document.

More importantly, **visa validity is not permitted stay length**. A valid multiple-entry visa may let you request admission on several separate trips during its validity, but a U.S. Customs and Border Protection officer decides whether you can enter and how long your stay is authorized each time. Your admission record (often available as an electronic I-94) matters. Do not assume each visit is automatically six months, and never confuse the visa expiration date with your permitted departure date.

Read both the official LINK[Morocco visa reciprocity schedule|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country/Morocco.html] and the LINK[meaning of U.S. visa expiration dates|https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-expiration-date.html]. Overstaying can seriously affect future eligibility.

QUOTE[A ten-year visa can permit repeated requests to enter. It is not permission to live in the United States for ten years.]

## 19. Avoid Visa Scams, Fake Appointments and Misleading Advice

Visa scams can appear as Facebook messages, WhatsApp offers, fake embassy accounts, paid 'guaranteed interview' appointments, invented sponsorship documents or websites with addresses that imitate official services. Be cautious whenever a person claims an inside connection at the consulate or promises a visa in exchange for a large payment. No legitimate agency can make a U.S. consular officer approve a visitor visa.

Check the address bar before entering passport numbers, birth dates or payment details. Use the official **ceac.state.gov** DS-160 form and navigate to local booking details through official government and visa-service resources. Never share an appointment login, passport scan or bank statement with unknown people online. If someone offers to complete a DS-160 for you, review every answer and make sure you retain control of your information.
